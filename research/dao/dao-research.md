# Basic concepts:

# Governance Structure Analysis

DAOs are not regulated in all countries over the world, for example Costa Rica has no regulations for lucrative actions. However,  the United States does have and demands a legal structure.

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission has declared that selling crypto tokens in a DAO requires registering them as securities and therefore subject to the federal security laws. The purpose of this registration provisions of the federal securities laws is to ensure that investors are sold investments that include all the proper disclosures and are subject to regulatory scrutiny for investor’s protection.

## Token distribution model for farmers and stakeholders

This section is crucial for a crypto project’s tokenomics as it determines the token allocation to different groups of investors. Remember that savvy investors analyze the fairness of the distribution structure. An ideal allocation has a small portion to insiders and the rest split among other groups.

Let’s see a breakdown of how projects typically distribute tokens initially:

- **Public**: People who invest in the crypto project through a public sale event.  
- **Community:** Enthusiasts who support the project idea and contribute to its growth. They may get tokens as rewards.  
- **Insiders:** Employees that receive tokens as an exchange.  
- **Foundation:** A non-profit entity that stewards a project and also that is able to get an allocation to fund future developments.

Once explained the balance of token distribution, it’s time to see the token distribution models. These models refer to the strategies employed to allocate a cryptocurrency’s tokens among various stakeholders, including developers, investors, advisors, and early participants.

It’s important to mention that the selected model can significantly impact a project’s long-term viability and success. And it’s a fact to say that they play a pivotal role on initial coin offerings (ICOs) or any decentralized project with an intrinsic token.

Commonly utilized token distribution models:

# Pre-Sale or Private Sale

The model involves selling a substantial portion of tokens to venture capital firms or pre-sale investors at an early stage, usually preceding a public sale. While securing financial stability and valuable connections from VC involvement, it may compromise token ownership decentralization.

# Public Sale

Tokens could be purchased by the public in general, typically following a well-publicized launch event. This model aims to achieve widespread token distribution.

# Airdrops

Tokens are freely distributed to holders of a specific blockchain’s native token. For example: Uniswap that made a massive airdrop with its token UNI.

# Proof of Stake or Staking

Token holders can “stake” their tokens in the network in exchange for additional tokens, voting power, or other benefits.

# Liquidity Mining or Yield Farming

Users provide liquidity to a decentralized exchange and receive tokens as rewards, often yielding returns surpassing traditional investment avenues.

# Community or Developer Rewards

Tokens are allocated to incentivize developers, early supporters, or community members to contribute to the project through bug fixes, feature additions, etc.

# Reserved for Future Use

Certain tokens are set aside for future needs like partnerships, community initiatives, or unforeseen network requirements.

# DAO Governance

Projects allocate tokens for a Decentralized Autonomous Organization (DAO), allowing token holders to vote on project development and fund utilization.

When choosing a token distribution, striking a balance among the interests of developers, investors, and users is essential for fostering a fair and sustainable ecosystem. It for sure will be critical, impacting, adoption and overall success.

Don’t you think there are only 9 ways for a distribution schema. There are more crypto projects finding smart ways to give out their tokens. Since tokens give holders rights and value in the project’s world, how they get spread around matters a lot.

Here some popular trends:

- **Tiered Buying:** Early buyers get better prices. This rewards risk-takers who buy in early. Later buyers pay more as the project gets valuable.  
- **Airdrops:** Dropping free token gifts into user wallets. This spreads the word fast. It also rewards loyal communities.  
- **Future Reserves:** Saving tokens for what's needed later like bug fixes and partners.   
- **Lock-Up Times:** Founders can't sell all tokens right away. This stops fast crashes in value.

Tips to create a successful token distribution strategy:

- Analyze project goals and needs to choose suitable models. Consider community incentives.  
- Determine the total token supply and allocation percentages for the distribution.  
- Create a transparent distribution schedule and process.  
- Market the project and token sale to attract ideal users and investors.   
- Launch on platforms that provide access to target communities.  
- Incentivize long-term holding to build stable value.

To wrap it up, the model for token distribution is a critical component of launching a successful crypto project. Various models exist, each with distinct pros and cons. Projects should analyze their needs and ideals to design an optimal overall distribution strategy. Getting the tokenomics right from the start gives the ecosystem the best chance to thrive.

## Voting mechanism design for escrow disputes

In a decentralized ecosystem, escrow contracts (warranty deposit) play a key role in guaranteeing sage transactions among parties that don’t trust each other. However, it’s subject to possible fights about retained funds. A fair and efficient voting system must follow some principles in order to avoid as much as possible any fight from one or multiple parties involved.

Key principles for Voting System design:

- **Impartial:** All parties involved must have access to a fair process without biases.  
- **Decentralization:** Every single decision must be taken in democracy, avoiding an authority and centralized entity.  
- **Transparency**: Every voter must be able to verify the results and assure that there’s no manipulation in the process.  
- **Security:** It must prevent attacks such as vote buying or decision manipulation.

**Common Voting Systems to solve disputes**

There are multiple ways to structure a voting mechanism that allows solving the dispute in a decentralized manner.

# Token-Weighted Voting

This model states that every voter must block their tokens in order to participate in the dispute resolution. Their votes have a proportional weight to the staking-tokens.  
Pros:

- It discourages manipulation, since voting requires a financial commitment.  
- It can reward honest voters with a share of the disputed funds.

Cons:

- It can favor large token holders (whales), centralizing decision-making.  
- Users with fewer tokens have less influence on decisions.

# Kleros-like System

This system establishes a random group of jurors selected to review the dispute. Jurors can be users with certain reputation criteria or who have locked up tokens to participate.  
Process flow:

1. A pool of N jurors is randomly selected.  
2. Each juror reviews the evidence presented by both sides.  
3. The jurors cast their votes independently.  
4. The verdict is determined by majority or supermajority.  
5. Jurors who voted with the majority receive rewards, while those who voted against lose a portion of their stake.

Pros:

- Reduces whales' power over decisions.  
- Decreases the possibility of corruption or manipulation.

Cons:

- It can be slow if the number of disputes grows.  
- It requires financial incentives for juries.

# Reputation-Based Voting

This model weights each vote based on each voter’s reputation within the ecosystem. Reputation can be measured by participation in the system, time in the community, and previous voting history.  
Pros:

- Favors committed users with a reliable history.  
- Reduces the influence of malicious voters or newcomers.

Cons:

- Difficult to implement if a good reputation system is not in place.  
- Can be exclusionary for new participants.

To ensure a secure and tamper-resistant voting process, there are strategies to proactively prevent it, however, if not possible to prevent, there must also be strategies to punish these kinds of actions. For example:  
 

- **Slashing Mechanism:** Financially penalizing those who participate in fraudulent decisions.  
- **Commit-Reveal Voting:** Votes are submitted in a hidden manner and only revealed at the end, avoiding the influence of others.  
- **Quadratic Voting:** Favors equitable participation by giving more power to the diversity of voters instead of the concentration of tokens.  
- **Time-Lock Voting:** A delay in the execution of the result to allow appeals if manipulation is detected.

The strategies selected for a voting system depends on the project’s nature, and also it’s possible to mix some of them in order to obtain a new strategy adapted to the project’s needs. It’s always important to have documentation about this particular process since it could bring clarity to new people and keep things clear among already participants.

## Proposal submission framework

A governance proposal is a tool for decision-making within a DAO. A well-defined framework ensures that proposals are structured, transparent and effectively evaluated by the community.

The main objective of a proposal submission is to provide a standardized method for DAO members to suggest improvements, introduce new features, or modify governance rules within the project.

The following points outline the key components of an efficient proposal submission process:

1. **Title**: A clear and concise title summarizing the essence of the proposed change.

2. **Executive summary:** An overview explaining:  
- **What** the proposal aims to achieve  
- **Why** it is necessary or beneficial  
- **How** it aligns with the DAO’s mission and long-term vision

3. **Problem Statement:** A detailed explanation of the issue the proposal intends to solve. This section should provide context, background information, and supporting data if available.  
4. **Proposed Solution:** A structured outline of the suggested solution including how it will be implemented, technical or operational requirements and expected challenges and mitigation strategies if necessary.  
5. **Stakeholders view analysis**: A breakdown of how the proposal will affect different stakeholders, including their benefits, potential risks and required resources (such as money, technical or human capital).

Some possible features to add a proposal that could be necessary in advanced stages:

1. **Implementation plan & timeline:** A clear roadmap detailing phases of execution, estimated timeframes and milestones.  
2. **Budget requirements:** If applicable, the proposal should specify the required budget, how the funds will be allocated and possible funding sources.  
3. **Accountability & monitoring:** A system to track the proposal’s implementation, which includes assigning tickets, KPIs and reporting frequency and format.

There’s a need also for a voting system that ensures participation but also time saving in order to keep the project moving forward. A voting system must include:

- **Voting method:** examples, token-weighted, quadratic, reputation-based, etc. Open Zepelin has a wizard to create simple voting systems: [Voting wizard](https://wizard.openzeppelin.com/)   
- **Quorum requirements:** this means the minimum participation needed.  
- **Approval criteria:** simple majority, supermajority, etc.

Finally, the submission and the review process. There must be an official way to submit a new proposal, the usual way is having an official DAO governance platform, that provides a form following the standard for a new proposal. The proposal is assigned a unique proposal ID and categorized accordingly.

The process continues with the governance committee (if applicable) or an automated validation system that ensures the proposal meets eligibility criteria and if everything’s okay, the proposal moves to the voting phase.

At this point, the community is able to vote using the designated mechanism within the established period. And if the proposal meets the required quorum and approval threshold it is **considered passed**.

Now, it’s time to implement and execute the proposal, so it should be transformed into the project’s implementation MO and the progress is shared with the community.

Last but not least, the DAO evaluates the effectiveness of the implemented proposal, adjustments made if necessary and a final report is published for transparency.

## Treasury management system

A proper management ensures financial stability, transparency, and equitable fund allocation. In a DAO-driven ecosystem, managing the treasury involves balancing decentralization, security, and efficient capital utilization.

There are some core principles for any DAO Treasury Management:

- All treasury activities should be visible to the community.  
- The treasury must maintain financial health to fund operations, grants, and ecosystem growth.  
- Funds should be allocated fairly, avoiding centralization of power or wealth.  
- Treasury funds must be safeguarded against hacks, mismanagement, or governance attacks.  
- While decentralized, the system should allow for agile decision-making when needed.

There are multiple ways to structure a DAO treasury, each with different levels of control, decentralization, and fairness. Here some of the most common structures:

1. **Multisig Wallets:** Where multiple DAO members must approve transactions. Ensures that no single person can access or move funds unilaterally.  
2. **Smart-contract based:** Funds are managed programmatically via smart contracts, following pre-defined rules. Can include automated fund distributions, staking mechanisms, or treasury diversification strategies.  
3. **Treasury committee**: A group of elected community members oversees treasury management based on governance votes. Often used alongside multisigs to execute community-approved spending.  
4. **Staking & Yield:** Some DAOs invest treasury funds in DeFi protocols to generate passive income. This ensures that the treasury remains sustainable while supporting long-term growth.

## Voting contract architecture

The voting system will be a core component of your DAO, enabling stakeholders to resolve disputes over escrow funds. Here’s a detailed architecture for the voting contract:

# Core Components of the Voting Contract

The voting contract will consist of the following key components:

## Proposal Creation:

A dispute is initiated when a buyer or farmer raises a claim (e.g., non-delivery, quality issues, etc.). The dispute is formalized as a proposal on the blockchain, containing:

- Dispute details (description, evidence, parties involved).  
- Escrow ID (linking the dispute to the specific escrow contract).  
- Voting options (e.g., "Release funds to farmer," "Refund buyer," "Split funds").

## Voting Mechanism:

A token-weighted voting system is used, where voting power is proportional to the number of governance tokens held by participants. Alternatively, a reputation-based system can be implemented, where voting power is tied to a user’s reputation score (e.g., based on their history of fair decisions).

## Voting Period:

A fixed time window (e.g., 7 days) is set for voting. Votes are recorded on-chain to ensure transparency and immutability.

## Quorum and Thresholds:

A quorum is required for the vote to be valid (e.g., at least 30% of eligible voters must participate). A supermajority threshold (e.g., 66%) is set to ensure broad consensus.

## Execution of Results:

Once the voting period ends, the contract automatically executes the outcome (e.g., releasing funds to the farmer or refunding the buyer). If the quorum or threshold is not met, the dispute is escalated to a secondary mechanism (e.g., a jury system like Kleros).

# Smart Contract Design

The voting contract will be implemented as a smart contract on the Stellar network. Here’s how it can be structured:

## Data Structures

- Proposal: Stores details of the dispute, including the escrow ID, description, voting options, and status.  
- Vote: Records each voter’s choice (e.g., "Yes," "No," "Abstain") and their voting power.  
- Escrow: Links the dispute to the escrow contract holding the funds.

## Functions

- createProposal(escrowID, description): Allows a user to initiate a dispute.  
- vote(proposalID, choice): Enables users to cast their votes.  
- executeProposal(proposalID): Executes the outcome of the vote once the voting period ends.  
- getResults(proposalID): Returns the current voting results for transparency.

## Security Features

- Time-locking: Prevents premature execution of the vote.  
- Slashing: Penalizes malicious voters (e.g., those who vote against the majority without valid reasoning).  
- Commit-Reveal Scheme: Ensures votes are submitted confidentially and revealed only after the voting period ends.

# Example Workflow

Here’s how the voting system would work in practice:

1. A buyer raises a dispute over the quality of delivered crops.  
2. A proposal is created on the blockchain, linking the dispute to the escrow contract.  
3. Stakeholders (e.g., farmers, buyers, community members) vote on the proposal using their governance tokens.  
4. After the voting period ends, the contract checks if the quorum and threshold are met  
5. If the vote passes, the funds are automatically released to the farmer or refunded to the buyer.  
6. If the vote fails, the dispute is escalated to a secondary mechanism (e.g., a jury system).
